Three treatments for the Sectors and Departments regions

Same live figures in each study: Sep 2026 YTD, all business units — 18 sectors, 92 cost centres. Click sectors and departments in any study; the selection filters exactly the way the page does, and the scope strip at the bottom of each frame shows what the chapter bands would then total.

A

Ledger Rows — one scale for every sector

Cards become ranked rows. The bar of each sector is drawn to one shared scale (Tourism's 1.72B is the full width, TeamLab's 80M a sliver) and split into Actual · Encumbrance · Fund — so relative size and consumption read in one glance, the way a board pack table does. Figures sit in aligned columns with their share of budget underneath.

  • Selecting = clicking the row; the check and a brand spine mark it. Others soften.
  • Departments use the same ledger, ranked by budget with the cost-centre code and sector under the name; top 12 shown, one click for all 92.
  • Best when the executive wants comparison and precision over pictures.
B

Composition Tiles — the cards, told in the bands' own colours

Keeps the card grid the page has today but gives each card a hierarchy: the budget as the one large figure, its share of the total, then a composition bar in the same Actual / Encumbrance / Fund colours as the chapter rings below, and a state pill that says how much room is left. The card and the band tell the same story in the same language.

  • Selected cards get a brand top edge and a filled check; a chip rail in the header lists the picks with one-click removal.
  • Department tiles carry the code, the sector tag and a compact bar; the header adds search and a sort (Budget · % used · % free).
  • Closest to what is live — an upgrade rather than a redesign.
C

Proportional Map — where the money sits, as one picture

Sectors become a treemap: tile area = budget share, fill = % consumed (Actual + Encumbrance), from pale for lightly-used to deep brand for nearly-committed and rust for over-committed. Departments are the same map nested inside their sectors. An executive sees the whole 8.48B and its pressure points before reading a single number.

  • Click a tile to select; picked tiles get a dark ring and tick, the rest fade. Small tiles keep a tooltip with the full figures.
  • Selecting sectors re-draws the department map to just those sectors, so their cost centres get the room.
  • The boldest of the three; the ledger row detail can still live one click away in the drill drawer.
